# Benchmark termination provenance

Three recent long benchmark controllers recorded external SIGTERM rather than their own deadline: direct-member forward, its replacement, and the declaration-cache screen. Their sender cannot be recovered from existing Node signal handlers. The latest declaration-cache interruption happened after about 156 seconds, well before its 45-minute deadline. Existing runs and partial samples remain archived and separate; interruption is not evidence of compiler failure or performance acceptance.

`supervise.py EXCLUSIVE_LOG -- COMMAND...` blocks SIGTERM/SIGINT/SIGHUP/SIGCHLD in a single-threaded parent and waits with sigwaitinfo. The child restores the original mask before exec. The parent records sender PID, UID, signal code and best-effort process comm (not command line/environment), immediately forwards cancellation to the child controller, and preserves its exit status. The benchmark controller remains responsible for its marked compiler-group cleanup. The supervisor neither retries nor suppresses cancellation and performs no periodic polling during execution. A separate child process group allows the controller to receive and coordinate forwarded cancellation.

A sender may exit before its comm can be read; the PID/code still record what the kernel supplied. PID namespaces may limit identity visibility. SIGKILL cannot be trapped. This does not identify the sender of earlier interruptions retroactively and must not be treated as a performance optimization.

Three tests passed: natural nonzero exit, SIGTERM sender identity/forwarding with child exit42, and exclusive-log preservation.
